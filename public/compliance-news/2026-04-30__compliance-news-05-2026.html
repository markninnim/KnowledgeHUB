Renters’ Rights Act 2025
30 April 2026

Background

This much talked about bill is taking effect in England on 1st May 2026.

You should all know about these changes but below is a summary anyway. This is important to understand, not just for your landlord clients, but also for any clients who are in rented accommodation and looking to buy a home.

Key Changes from 1st May 2026

  • End of 'No-fault' Evictions: Landlords cannot evict tenants without a valid, legal reason.
  • Periodic Tenancies: Fixed-term contracts are replaced by monthly, rolling, or weekly tenancies.
  • Ban on Rental Bidding: It is a legal offence for landlords/agents to encourage or accept higher bids than the advertised rent.
  • Rent Increases: Limited to once per year, restricted to market rates, and requires a formal Section 13 notice.
  • Pets & Discrimination: Landlords cannot unreasonably deny requests for pets and are banned from discriminating against tenants with children or on benefits.
  • Standards & Safety: The Decent Homes Standard is extended to the private rental sector.
  • Ombudsman & Database: A new Private Rented Sector Landlord Ombudsman will resolve disputes, and all landlords must register on a national database.

Impact on Current Tenants

  • Existing Tenancies: Assured Shorthold Tenancies (ASTs) signed before 1st May 2026 will transition to the new, rolling system – APTs (Periodic) not ASTs.
  • Notice Periods: Tenants can leave with two months' notice, whilst landlords must provide longer, justified notice periods.

Action

Please ensure you fully understand these changes so that you can advise your landlord clients, and potential first time buyers leaving a rented property, where necessary.
