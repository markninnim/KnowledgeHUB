Compliance News 15/2026

Guardian dual life protection applications
15 September 2026

Protection Advisers only.

Background

When submitting one application to Guardian with life cover for two lives, the clients have dual life cover. The same applies for critical illness policies. This means each client effectively has their own life cover policy (or two single life policies within one joint life application).

Historically, we were told by Guardian that these policies were "cross-proposed", like all other insurers, meaning each single life policy would be jointly owned by the two plan holders (or applicants).

We have now discovered this is incorrect. These are in fact two separate legal contracts, each owned only by the individual who is covered under it — i.e. Mr Smith owns his policy solely, and Mrs Smith owns her policy solely, despite the cover being arranged on one joint application.

Why this matters

Where the individual policies are solely owned by each single life assured, and one life assured dies, the claim will not automatically be paid to the surviving life assured. Instead, the proceeds will be paid out as per Probate where a Will is in place, or in accordance with Intestacy Law where there is no Will. This does not automatically give the surviving life assured an entitlement to the policy proceeds.

Most of our protection is arranged as mortgage protection, and in these instances our Suitability Reports say that in the event of death, the proceeds can be used to repay the mortgage — but this might not necessarily be the case.

For example, in an unmarried couple with no Will in place, if one partner were to die the proceeds would be paid to the deceased's family, not their partner with whom they have a joint mortgage debt. Should this happen, it is likely to result in a complaint, and could require us to compensate the surviving client by repaying their mortgage for them.

Important actions on future cases

With immediate effect, you must now complete the "pay out planner" at the point of completing the online application form with the clients.

The pay out planner simply requires you to name the person(s) who should be paid the proceeds in the event of death. Irrespective of your client's marital status, for mortgage cases the proceeds should be paid to the other applicant so they can use the proceeds to repay their joint mortgage debt. So, in the example of Mr & Mrs Smith, Mr Smith should be shown to benefit from Mrs Smith's policy, and Mrs Smith from Mr Smith's policy.

Guardian will pay the proceeds in accordance with the pay out planner unless the clients subsequently complete a trust form, in which case the trust form will supersede any previous instructions.

What about existing Guardian policies?

We are working with Guardian to find a solution for all existing policies already in force. We have a list of cases where neither a pay out planner nor a trust form has been completed, and will update you as soon as we know more about what needs to be actioned on these cases.

Further information

This communication concerns Guardian policies only, where the client has two single life policies from one joint application. For all other insurers, the legal status is that both plan holders jointly own any individual life policies, meaning on death the proceeds will be paid to the surviving life assured, as they would in a joint life situation.
